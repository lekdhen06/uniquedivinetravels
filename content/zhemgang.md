Zhemgang is a district located in the southern part of central Bhutan. It is one of the country’s more remote regions and is known for its rich forests, wildlife, and natural landscapes.
The district is less visited than many other parts of Bhutan, offering a quieter and more traditional experience.

## Geography

Zhemgang is characterized by hilly and mountainous terrain covered with dense subtropical and tropical forests.
The region forms part of the Eastern Himalayan ecological zone and supports a wide variety of plant life.

## Biodiversity

The district is known for its high biodiversity and natural habitats.
Zhemgang lies close to important protected areas, including Royal Manas National Park, which supports diverse wildlife species such as elephants, tigers, and many bird species.

## Culture

Zhemgang is home to several indigenous communities whose lifestyles are closely connected to nature.
Local people rely on agriculture, animal husbandry, and traditional practices, preserving long-established Bhutanese customs.

## Attractions

The district offers opportunities to explore untouched forests, rural villages, and monastic sites.
Visitors can experience the peaceful countryside and observe traditional lifestyles largely unchanged by modernization.

## Climate

Zhemgang experiences a subtropical climate with warm and humid summers.
Winters are milder, and the region receives significant rainfall during the monsoon season.

## Accessibility

Due to its rugged terrain and remote location, access to Zhemgang can be challenging.
The district is connected to other parts of Bhutan mainly through road links from neighboring districts.

Zhemgang is an off-the-beaten-path destination that highlights Bhutan’s natural beauty and cultural simplicity. It is well suited for travelers seeking wilderness, biodiversity, and authentic rural experiences.
