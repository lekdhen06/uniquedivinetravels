Paro is a picturesque town in western Bhutan, located not far from the capital city, Thimphu. It is renowned for its natural beauty, cultural landmarks, and as the main international gateway to the country.

## Airport

Paro is home to Bhutan’s only international airport. The airport is famous for its challenging approach, as it lies in a narrow valley surrounded by high mountains.
Only a limited number of specially trained pilots are certified to land here.

## Natural beauty

Paro lies in a fertile valley through which the Paro Chhu river flows. The region is surrounded by forested hills and sacred sites.
One of the most iconic landmarks is the Taktsang Monastery, also known as Tiger’s Nest, perched dramatically on a cliff above the valley.

## Cultural attractions

Paro has a rich cultural heritage with several important religious and historical sites.
Rinpung Dzong, also known as Paro Dzong, serves as both an administrative and monastic center. The annual Paro Tshechu festival is also held here.

## Traditional architecture

Paro is known for its traditional Bhutanese architecture, featuring intricately designed buildings, colorful wooden facades, and ornate decorations.
The town reflects Bhutan’s commitment to preserving its cultural identity.

## Tourism

Paro is a popular destination for visitors to Bhutan. The area offers hiking, trekking, bird-watching, and cultural sightseeing.
The hike to Taktsang Monastery is one of the most popular activities for travelers.

## Agriculture

The Paro Valley is known for its fertile agricultural land. Farmers cultivate crops such as rice, apples, and other fruits using both traditional and modern farming methods.

## Climate

Paro experiences a temperate climate with distinct seasons. Summers are generally mild, while winters can be cold, with snowfall occurring in higher elevations.

Paro offers a blend of natural beauty, cultural richness, and traditional lifestyle. It provides visitors with a deep insight into Bhutan’s heritage and mountainous landscapes.
